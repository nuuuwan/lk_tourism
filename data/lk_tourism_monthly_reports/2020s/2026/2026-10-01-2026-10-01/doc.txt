Monthly Tourist Arrivals Report October 2026

Introduction The following document provides a synthesis of the most recent October 2026 international visitor arrivals data sourced from the Department of Immigration & Emigration. The data is current as of 31st October 2026, and subject to change. The data provides an overview of month on month and annual changes in visitor arrivals to Sri Lanka from international source markets. The data is meant to provide an overview of how travel and tourism is performing to Sri Lanka. This report is developed by the Research & International Relations Division at Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority (SLTDA). Questions, comments and feedback are welcome and will support the future amendment and enhancement of the report to ensure it meets the data and insights needs of Sri Lanka’s Government and industry stakeholders.

Monthly Tourist Arrivals Report October 2026

Summary

Sri Lanka recorded a total of 1,862,304 tourist arrivals during January–October 2026, compared with 1,890,687 arrivals during the corresponding period in 2025. This represents a marginal decline of 1.5%, equivalent to 28,383 fewer arrivals year-on-year. Despite the fluctuations experienced during the year, the cumulative performance indicates that Sri Lanka has remained relatively close to the previous year's arrival levels.

October 2026 recorded an encouraging improvement, with 168,625 tourist arrivals, compared with 165,193 in October 2025, representing a year-on-year increase of 2.1%. More notably, October 2026 recorded the highest number of tourist arrivals ever recorded for the month of October, surpassing the previous October record of 153,123 arrivals registered in 2018. This achievement is particularly significant given the challenges faced by the tourism industry during the year.

The October performance also marks a return to positive year-on-year growth following several months of declines. Although the increase is relatively modest, it indicates an improvement in

visitor arrivals towards the latter part of the year. The fact that October arrivals exceeded the previous year's figure and established a new monthly record reflects the continued resilience of Sri Lanka's tourism sector.

India, the United Kingdom, China, Australia and Germany emerged as the key source markets for Sri Lanka in October 2026, highlighting the continued importance of these markets in supporting the country's tourism performance. According to the September 2026 UN Tourism World Tourism Barometer, the forecast for international tourist arrivals growth in 2026 has been revised downwards from 3%–4% to 1%–2%, reflecting geopolitical uncertainties, particularly the Middle East conflict, and its potential impact on oil prices, travel costs and global inflation. The report also highlights a shift in travel demand towards nearby and more accessible destinations, as travellers increasingly prioritise value for money and opt for closer-to-home or domestic travel amid rising costs and uncertainty.

The regional distribution of tourist arrivals to Sri Lanka in October 2026 highlights the continued dominance of the Asia and Pacific region, which accounted for the largest share of international visitors. Out of the total 168,625 arrivals recorded during the month, 104,453 visitors (61.9%) originated from this region, reinforcing its importance as the principal source region for Sri Lanka's tourism industry.

Europe remained the second-largest source region in October 2026, accounting for 53,721 arrivals (31.9%) of the total. Together, Europe and Asia and Pacific contributed 93.8% of all tourist arrivals, highlighting Sri Lanka's continued dependence on these two key regions. However, the European market faces several challenges, with widespread demonstrations and social unrest reported in countries such as France, Italy, Belgium and Portugal during October, largely driven by rising living costs, housing affordability and wage pressures. Coupled with persistent inflation and higher energy costs, these developments may place further pressure on household disposable incomes and influence overseas travel decisions. As a result, European travellers may become increasingly price-conscious, favouring shorter holidays, postponing long-haul trips or opting for more affordable destinations. Such changing travel behaviour could potentially affect demand for Sri Lanka from European source markets.

The Americas contributed 6,839 visitors, representing 4.1% of total arrivals, while the Middle East accounted for 2,223 visitors (1.3%). Africa recorded the smallest contribution, with 1,389 arrivals, representing 0.8% of the total.

The regional distribution indicates that Sri Lanka's tourism demand remains largely concentrated in the Asian and European markets. The substantial contribution from Asia and the Pacific highlights the importance of maintaining strong regional connectivity and market engagement, particularly with established markets such as India and China. Top ten source markets

January to October 2026

Sri Lanka's tourism performance during January–October 2026 reflects a mixed pattern across its major source markets, with growth concentrated in selected markets, particularly India, China and Australia, while several traditional European markets and Russia recorded notable declines.

The country recorded a total of 1,862,304 tourist arrivals, compared with 1,890,687 arrivals during the corresponding period in 2025, representing an overall decline of approximately 1.5%. Despite the contraction in total arrivals, the performance of several Asian and regional markets indicates a continuing shift in the composition of Sri Lanka's tourism source markets.

India continued to dominate as Sri Lanka's largest source market, recording 499,625 arrivals, an increase of 18.0% compared with the corresponding period in 2025. India accounted for approximately 26.8% of total arrivals, reinforcing its importance to Sri Lanka's tourism industry. This performance highlights the resilience of the Indian market and its significant contribution to sustaining tourist arrivals amid declining demand from several other key markets.

China retained its position as the third-largest source market, recording 123,740 arrivals, reflecting a 10.0% increase. Similarly, Australia recorded a positive growth of 12.0%, with 95,498 arrivals. These increases indicate improving demand from selected Asian and long-haul markets and provide some balance against the weaker performance of other traditional markets.

In contrast, Russia recorded the steepest decline among the top ten source markets, with arrivals falling by 32.8% from 133,640 to 89,741. This represents a substantial reduction in arrivals from a market that has traditionally made an important contribution to Sri Lanka's tourism industry. The Netherlands also experienced a considerable decline of 22.0%, followed by Bangladesh (-26.3%), France (-10.1%) and Germany (-9.8%). These developments suggest that the contraction in arrivals from several European and regional markets has weighed on overall tourism performance.

The United Kingdom, Sri Lanka's second-largest source market, recorded a marginal decline of 1.0%, with 173,129 arrivals. Despite this slight reduction, the UK continued to demonstrate relative stability compared with the more pronounced declines observed in Germany, France, Russia and the Netherlands. The United States also recorded a moderate decline of 5.2%, with 49,435 arrivals.

Although Sri Lanka recorded a marginal contraction in overall tourist arrivals during the first ten months of 2026, the performance was not uniformly negative across source markets. Growth in India, China and Australia provided some support to the industry, while significant declines in Russia and several European markets constrained overall performance. The results underline the importance of strengthening existing high-performing markets while revitalising demand from declining traditional markets. A more balanced source-market portfolio would help improve the resilience and sustainability of Sri Lanka's tourism industry.

Tourist arrivals by purpose of visit October 2026

The distribution of tourist arrivals by purpose of visit in October 2026 indicates that

Pleasure/Vacation remained the dominant travel purpose, accounting for 121,814 visitors or 72.24% of total arrivals (168,625). This highlights the continued importance of leisure tourism as the principal driver of international visitor demand for Sri Lanka. Visiting Friends and Relatives (VFR) was the second-largest category, recording 16,340

visitors (9.69%), followed closely by Meetings, Incentives, Conferences and Exhibitions (MICE), which attracted 15,505 visitors (9.19%). The relatively significant share of MICE arrivals reflects the contribution of business events and professional engagements to the country's tourism industry, highlighting opportunities to further develop Sri Lanka as a regional MICE destination. Business-related travel accounted for 4,833 arrivals (2.87%). Health/Ayurvedic tourism recorded 1,376 visitors, representing 0.82% of total arrivals.

Although this segment accounted for a relatively small share of the market, it highlights the continued demand for Sri Lanka's traditional healing and wellness experiences. Sports-related travel contributed 876 visitors (0.52%), followed by Education (770 visitors; 0.46%) and Religious tourism (609 visitors; 0.36%). Overall, the October 2026 arrival profile demonstrates a strong concentration of visitor

demand in leisure tourism, which accounted for nearly three-quarters of total arrivals. At the same time, the combined contribution of VFR, MICE and business travel indicates the importance of diversifying Sri Lanka's tourism offering beyond conventional leisure travel. Strengthening niche segments such as wellness, sports, education and religious tourism could provide opportunities to broaden the visitor base, reduce reliance on a single travel purpose and enhance the resilience and year-round competitiveness of the tourism sector. An interesting feature of the October arrival profile is the relatively high contribution of MICE tourism from the Russian Federation, where 26.73% of arrivals were recorded under this purpose. This was considerably higher than the corresponding proportions in other major source markets. India (9.32%), Germany (7.38%) and France (11.01%) also recorded notable MICE-related shares. These findings suggest opportunities to further strengthen Sri Lanka's position in the international business events market through targeted engagement with countries demonstrating an interest in MICE travel.

Health and Ayurvedic tourism displayed a particularly distinctive market concentration. Germany recorded the highest proportion of visitors travelling for health and Ayurvedic purposes, at 7.80%, substantially exceeding the corresponding shares in other markets. The proportions recorded in the Russian Federation (0.44%), France (0.42%) and the United Kingdom (0.23%) were comparatively modest. This reinforces Germany's importance as a potential priority market for promoting Sri Lanka's traditional healing systems, Ayurveda-based treatments and wellness experiences.

Business travel accounted for relatively small shares across the major source markets, although the United States (4.83%) and India (3.33%) recorded comparatively higher proportions. Sports tourism remained a relatively minor travel purpose across all markets, with the United Kingdom (0.95%) and the United States (0.85%) recording the highest shares. Educational and religious travel also represented relatively small segments, with no substantial concentration observed across the markets examined.

Overall, the findings demonstrate that Sri Lanka's major source markets differ considerably in their travel motivations, despite the dominance of leisure tourism. While China, Bangladesh and India exhibit a strong concentration in holiday travel, markets such as the United Kingdom, Australia and the United States offer opportunities to attract visitors through VFR and other travel segments. The relatively high MICE share from the Russian Federation and the distinctive concentration of health and Ayurvedic visitors from Germany further illustrate the importance of market-specific promotional approaches. These differences provide useful insights for refining international marketing strategies, developing specialised tourism products and expanding the contribution of niche segments to Sri Lanka's tourism industry.

The Rise of the Kidfluencers: A New Generation Shaping Family Travel

Family travel is evolving, with children increasingly taking an active role in deciding where families travel, what they do and how they spend their holidays. Recent research by Tripadvisor highlights this changing trend, showing that children are no longer simply accompanying their parents on family vacations but are becoming influential participants in the travel planning process. Described as “kidfluencers”, these young travellers are shaping family holiday choices through their interests, preferences and expectations.

This trend is also reflected in Sri Lanka's inbound tourism market. According to the SLTDA Survey on Inbound Tourists conducted during 2024/25, 41% of the 11,731 international visitors surveyed travelled with their families. Family travel was more prevalent among visitors from the United Kingdom, UAE, Switzerland, Greece, Spain, Saudi Arabia, Russia, Romania, Poland, New Zealand, the Netherlands, Lebanon, Kazakhstan, Jordan, Italy, Iran, Hungary, France, the Czech Republic, Belgium, Belarus and Australia. This pattern suggests a stronger preference among visitors from these markets for shared leisure experiences, which may include family holidays and multi-generational travel. The findings highlight the relevance of the family travel segment to Sri Lanka's inbound tourism market and the importance of understanding the needs and expectations of different family groups.

Tripadvisor's research further highlights the growing influence of children in family travel decisions. Around 80% of children aged 1–17 have an influence on where their families travel, while 42% are identified as the main influence. This involvement begins at an early age, with approximately 70% of children participating in the initial inspiration and research stages of trip planning. By the age of seven, around four in ten children are already influencing family travel choices, increasing to almost two-thirds by the age of ten. This suggests that travel preferences are being shaped from a much younger age than traditionally assumed.

Children's influence is also reflected in the types of activities included in family itineraries. Tripadvisor's research indicates that Viator has recorded a 50% increase in bookings for child-related experiences, including city tours and visits to aquariums. Theme parks remain a popular choice among children across age groups, with an average preference of 52%, followed by beach experiences at 47.5%. However, preferences vary according to age. Children aged 0–7 show a greater interest in nature and wildlife experiences (43%), while those aged 8–13 are more attracted to active adventures (38%). Teenagers aged 14–17 tend to favour urban exploration, city trips and sightseeing (33%). These differences demonstrate the importance of offering a diverse range of activities that cater to different age groups and interests.

Popular entertainment and digital media are also playing an increasingly important role in influencing family travel choices. Around 29% of parents indicate that television programmes, films and video games influence their decisions on where to travel. This figure rises to 36% among families with children aged 8–10. Characters, destinations and experiences featured in popular entertainment, including Bluey, Minecraft and K-pop-related content, can therefore become sources of inspiration for family holidays.

Another notable trend is the increasing importance of advance planning. Family holidays are closely linked to school calendars, making early booking an important consideration for parents. According to the research, 41% of families book their holidays at least four months in advance, while only 12% tend to make last-minute travel and experience bookings. Families increasingly prefer confirmed itineraries, pre-arranged activities and convenient travel arrangements rather than leaving decisions until arrival.

Despite efforts to manage holiday expenses, children's preferences can also influence family spending. Approximately 83% of parents reported spending more than originally planned due to requests from their children, while 25% said this happens frequently. This indicates that families may be willing to increase their spending to accommodate activities and experiences that create enjoyable memories for their children. At the same time, familiarity and positive past experiences remain important, with 73% of parents indicating that they return to destinations or types of holidays they have previously enjoyed. Interestingly, family travel is not limited to parents and children, as 47% of children have also travelled independently with their grandparents.

While children increasingly influence the choice of destinations and activities, parents continue to place considerable importance on convenience, safety and comfort. Direct flights, flexible cancellation arrangements, clean accommodation, convenient locations and breakfast-inclusive packages are among the factors that influence family travel decisions. Facilities such as swimming pools designed for children, family rooms, safe play areas and child-friendly dining options are also important. For families with younger children, easy access to familiar food and secure recreational facilities can make a significant difference, while older children and teenagers may be more interested in cooking classes, night markets, guided adventures and opportunities to explore local culture.

Food is another essential component of the family travel experience. Many families prefer a combination of dining at restaurants and preparing meals at their accommodation. This provides greater flexibility, helps manage expenses and makes mealtimes easier, particularly when travelling with young children who may have specific food preferences.

Overall, these findings point to an important shift in the family travel market. Children are becoming active participants in travel decisions, while parents increasingly seek experiences that balance the interests of all family members. For tourism destinations and service providers, this presents an opportunity to develop more inclusive and age-appropriate tourism products, supported by convenient travel arrangements, family-friendly accommodation and engaging experiences.

For Sri Lanka, the findings of the SLTDA inbound tourist survey, together with the emerging global trends identified by Tripadvisor, highlight the potential to further strengthen the family tourism segment. The country's diverse tourism offering provides opportunities to develop attractive family holiday experiences by combining beach holidays, wildlife and nature experiences, cultural attractions, adventure activities and interactive learning opportunities. Wildlife safaris, visits to national parks, boat rides, cooking demonstrations, cultural excursions and nature-based activities can appeal to different age groups while allowing families to explore the destination together. Greater attention to family-friendly accommodation, safe transport, convenient facilities and well-organised itineraries could further enhance Sri Lanka's appeal to international family travellers.

It is about creating memorable experiences that bring families together, meet the expectations of different generations and encourage visitors to return. As the influence of young travellers continues to grow, destinations and tourism businesses that recognise and respond to their preferences will be better positioned to capture this evolving market.

Monthly Tourist Arrivals Report October 2026

Main last departure airports and airlines to Sri Lanka,

October 2026

An analysis of tourist arrivals by last port of departure in October 2026 highlights the importance of regional air connectivity and major international transit hubs in facilitating travel to Sri Lanka. Chennai recorded the highest number of arrivals, with 16,584 visitors, accounting for 9.83% of total arrivals. Bangalore followed with 13,301 visitors (7.89%), while Doha ranked third with 11,957 visitors (7.09%). Mumbai also contributed a significant number of arrivals, recording 11,846 visitors (7.03%). The prominence of these Indian departure points reflects the importance of India's geographical proximity, strong air connectivity and its position as one of Sri Lanka's principal source markets. Major Middle Eastern aviation hubs also played a significant role in connecting international travellers to Sri Lanka. Doha, Dubai and Abu Dhabi collectively accounted for 34,509 arrivals, representing approximately 20.46% of the total. Istanbul contributed a further 6,961 visitors (4.13%), while Sharjah recorded 2,902 arrivals (1.72%). These figures underline the importance of Middle Eastern airports as key transit gateways, particularly for travellers originating from Europe and other international markets. Other important departure points included Delhi (10,870; 6.45%), Hyderabad (6,692; 3.97%), London (6,151; 3.65%), Kuala Lumpur (5,980; 3.55%) and Singapore (5,340; 3.17%). The contribution from these cities reflects Sri Lanka's connectivity with key markets across South Asia, Europe and Southeast Asia. Arrivals from Male (4,062; 2.41%), Hong Kong (3,506; 2.08%), Melbourne (3,358; 1.99%) and Moscow (3,358; 1.99%) further demonstrate the geographical diversity of international air connections serving the destination. Overall, the arrival pattern demonstrates the strategic importance of India's major cities and Middle Eastern transit hubs in supporting Sri Lanka's international air connectivity. The strong contribution from Chennai, Bangalore, Mumbai and Delhi highlights the importance of maintaining and further strengthening direct air links with India. At the same time, the significant role played by Doha, Dubai and Abu Dhabi reinforces the importance of maintaining strong connectivity through Gulf aviation hubs, particularly for long-haul markets. Continued engagement with international airlines, expansion of direct flight connections and strengthening of regional air links will therefore remain important in improving accessibility and supporting the sustained growth of Sri Lanka's tourism industry.

Monthly Tourist Arrivals Report October 2026

The distribution of tourist arrivals by airline in October 2026 highlights the important role played by both national and international carriers in facilitating visitor movements to Sri Lanka. Sri Lankan Airlines remained the leading carrier, bringing in 48,356 visitors and accounting for 28.68% of total arrivals. This reinforces the airline's position as the principal air transport provider serving the country's international tourism market and highlights its importance in maintaining Sri Lanka's connectivity with key source markets.

IndiGo ranked second, recording 29,778 arrivals (17.66%), reflecting the strong air travel links between Sri Lanka and India. Together, Sri Lankan Airlines and IndiGo accounted for 46.34% of total arrivals, indicating that nearly half of all visitors entered the country through these two carriers. This also demonstrates the importance of the Indian market and the extensive air connectivity between the two neighbouring countries.

Middle Eastern airlines also accounted for a considerable share of arrivals. Qatar Airways recorded 12,040 visitors (7.14%), followed by Etihad Airways with 10,473 (6.21%) and Emirates with 8,715 (5.17%). Flydubai and Air Arabia contributed a further 4,079 (2.42%) and 2,908 (1.72%) arrivals, respectively. Collectively, these five carriers accounted for approximately 22.66% of total arrivals. Their contribution highlights the importance of Gulf aviation hubs, particularly Doha, Abu Dhabi, Dubai and Sharjah, in connecting Sri Lanka with major international markets, including Europe and other long-haul destinations.

Among other international carriers, Air India recorded 9,117 arrivals (5.41%), while Turkish Airlines contributed 6,961 (4.13%). China Eastern Airlines and Singapore Airlines accounted for 4,007 (2.38%) and 3,871 (2.30%) arrivals, respectively. Cathay Pacific (2.08%) and Aeroflot (1.76%) also made notable contributions. The presence of these carriers reflects the diversity of Sri Lanka's international air connectivity across South Asia, East Asia, Southeast Asia, Europe and the Middle East.

Other airlines collectively accounted for 11,765 arrivals, representing 6.98% of the total. This indicates that, in addition to the major carriers, a wider network of international airlines also contributes to bringing visitors to Sri Lanka.

Overall, the findings demonstrate Sri Lanka's reliance on a combination of its national carrier, regional airlines and major international transit carriers to sustain international visitor arrivals. While Sri Lankan Airlines continues to play a leading role, the significant contribution of IndiGo highlights the importance of the Indian aviation market. Similarly, the strong presence of Middle Eastern carriers underlines the country's dependence on Gulf hubs for access to several long-haul markets. Maintaining strong partnerships with international airlines, expanding direct air services and improving connectivity with emerging source markets will therefore be important in supporting the continued growth and diversification of Sri Lanka's tourism industry.

The airline distribution data for October reveals interesting differences in passenger preferences across the various markets.
